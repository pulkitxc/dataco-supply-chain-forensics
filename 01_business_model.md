**# Phase 06.01 – Business System Discovery**



**---**



**# Business Goal**



**Design an analytical data warehouse that enables DataCo to answer business questions related to:**



**- Sales Performance**

**- Profitability**

**- Customer Behavior**

**- Product Performance**

**- Shipping Performance**

**- Regional Performance**



**The warehouse should provide a single source of truth for analytical reporting and decision-making.**



**---**



**# Business Story**



**The business operates through a sequence of events.**



**Customer**

**↓**



**Places an Order**

**↓**



**Order contains one or more Products**

**↓**



**Order is processed**

**↓**



**Order is shipped**

**↓**



**Order is delivered**



**Every completed purchase generates business measurements such as sales, profit, quantity, discount, and shipping performance.**



**---**



**# First Principle**



**The analytical model separates the business into two fundamental concepts.**



**## 1. Independent Business Entities**



**These exist regardless of whether a sale happens.**



**Examples:**



**- Customer**

**- Product**

**- Date**



**These become \*\*Dimension Tables\*\* because they describe the business.**



**---**



**## 2. Business Event**



**The business event represents something that happens.**



**For DataCo, the business event is:**



**> A customer purchases one product within one order.**



**This event generates measurable business outcomes such as revenue, profit, quantity, and shipping performance.**



**This becomes the \*\*Fact Table\*\*.**



**---**



**# Analytical Grain**



**## Grain Definition**



**One row represents:**



**> One customer purchasing one product within one order.**



**This is also known as an \*\*Order Line Item\*\*.**



**---**



**## Why this Grain?**



**A single order may contain multiple products.**



**Each product can have:**



**- Different Quantity**

**- Different Discount**

**- Different Sales**

**- Different Profit**



**Therefore, one order cannot represent one analytical record.**



**The smallest meaningful business event is one order line item.**



**---**



**# Independent Business Entities**



**After analyzing the business and dataset, the analytical warehouse contains three independent entities.**



**## Customer**



**Represents the people or organizations purchasing products.**



**Answers:**



**- Who bought?**



**---**



**## Product**



**Represents products sold by DataCo.**



**Answers:**



**- What was purchased?**



**---**



**## Date**



**Represents the business calendar used for time-based analysis.**



**Answers:**



**- When did the sale happen?**



**---**



**# Business Event**



**The Fact Table stores business events rather than business entities.**



**Each row represents one completed order line item together with its business measurements.**



**The Fact Table answers:**



**- What happened?**

**- How much was sold?**

**- How profitable was the sale?**

**- How was the order delivered?**



**---**



**# Facts vs Dimensions**



**## Facts (Business Measurements)**



**Facts are numerical values generated by a business event.**



**Examples:**



**- Sales**

**- Order Profit**

**- Benefit per Order**

**- Quantity**

**- Discount**

**- Order Item Total**

**- Shipping Days**



**These values are analyzed to measure business performance.**



**---**



**## Dimensions (Business Context)**



**Dimensions describe the business entities involved in the event.**



**### Customer**



**Who bought?**



**### Product**



**What was bought?**



**### Date**



**When did it happen?**



**Dimensions provide context for interpreting business measurements.**



**---**



**# Why Shipping and Location Are Not Separate Dimensions**



**During data modeling, Shipping and Location were evaluated as potential dimensions.**



**However, the dataset showed that:**



**Shipping information such as:**



**- Shipping Mode**

**- Delivery Status**

**- Shipping Days**

**- Late Delivery Risk**



**changes for every order.**



**Similarly,**



**Order Location:**



**- Order City**

**- Order Country**

**- Order Region**



**describes where that specific order was delivered rather than describing an independent business object.**



**Because these attributes belong to individual business events, they remain inside the Fact Table instead of becoming separate dimensions.**



**---**



**# Relationships**



**Each Dimension has its own unique identity (Primary Key).**



**The Fact Table stores business events.**



**Instead of repeating customer, product, and date information, it stores references (Foreign Keys) to the corresponding dimensions.**



**This creates:**



**- One Source of Truth**

**- Reduced Data Redundancy**

**- Better Data Integrity**

**- Simpler Analytical Queries**



**---**



**# Final Star Schema**



&#x20;               **dim\_customer**

&#x20;                     **│**

&#x20;                     **│**

**dim\_date ───── fact\_sales ───── dim\_product**



**The Fact Table acts as the center of the analytical model.**



**Dimensions provide business context.**



**Facts provide business measurements.**



**Together they create a scalable analytical warehouse for SQL analysis and Power BI reporting.**
